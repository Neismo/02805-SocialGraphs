What we asked

Wikipedia's Marvel roster links to itself 1,784 times. So: who does the universe point at, who does the pointing, and is anybody sitting outside it altogether?

What we did. Loaded the frozen week-1 snapshot as a directed graph — all 303 characters added before any edge, so the isolated ones survive — then looked at the in- and out-degree distributions on linear and log–log axes, compared the two leaderboards, and pulled apart the connected components. As a check on the data itself we re-derived one corner of the edge list from the live Wikipedia API.

The short answer. The universe points at Spider-Man, and it is not close: 35% of the other characters link to his article. But he is not the one doing the pointing — that is Betsy Braddock, who links out to 28 characters and is linked to by 7. Being famous and being talkative turn out to be almost unrelated properties, and one 9-character corner of the roster is not connected to the rest of it at all.

1. Data Loading & Graph Construction in Python

The graph is constructed using NetworkX (DiGraph) and Pandas from the frozen snapshot TSV datasets (week1_nodes.tsv and week1_edges.tsv).

load_week1_graph.py 
import pandas as pd, networkx as nx

nodes = pd.read_csv("week1_nodes.tsv", sep="\t", comment="#",
                    quoting=3)  # QUOTE_NONE: TSVs never quote, but blurbs may contain "quotes"
edges = pd.read_csv("week1_edges.tsv", sep="\t", comment="#",
                    names=["source", "target"])

G = nx.DiGraph()
G.add_nodes_from(nodes.node_id)
G.add_edges_from(edges.itertuples(index=False))
# G: 303 nodes, 1784 edges
💡 Key Implementation Details:
  • quoting=3 corresponds to csv.QUOTE_NONE, preventing unescaped double quotes inside character descriptions from breaking parsing.
  • comment="#" strips header annotation lines from the dataset.
  • 17 characters have zero incoming and outgoing links in this snapshot (isolated nodes), but are retained in the full node set via G.add_nodes_from(nodes.node_id).

The in-degree distribution is the heavy-tailed one: most characters are linked to a handful of times and a few are linked to by a third of the roster. Out-degree is far more even — nobody links out to more than 28 characters, because an article can only be so long. The two distributions have the same mean (5.89) and they must: every edge is one character's out-link and another's in-link, so the averages are forced to agree. Only the shapes differ.

3. In versus Out — Two Different Sets of People

Who is linked to most, and who links out most? Ranking the same 303 characters by the two directions gives two largely different lists.

# Linked to most (by in-degree) Links out most (by out-degree)
CharacterInOut CharacterInOut

Why are they different people? The two directions measure different things. In-degree is a verdict passed by everyone else: it counts how often the rest of the roster found this character worth naming, so it tracks fame inside the fictional universe. Out-degree is a property of one article's prose: it counts how many other roster members this page happens to mention, which depends on how long the article is and whether some editor wrote a sprawling "other versions" or "relationships" section. Betsy Braddock tops the out list because her body-swap history is a tangle that name-checks half the X-Men, not because she is the most important character in Marvel.

The two are correlated across the whole roster — Spearman's rank correlation is 0.70, so on average the well-connected are well-connected both ways — but the correlation breaks down exactly at the top, where the hubs live. That is the useful lesson: an aggregate correlation can be strong and still tell you nothing about the extremes you actually care about.

The one real island is Strikeforce: Morituri — characters from a 1986 limited series that shares no cast with the rest of Marvel. Nine characters, 22 links among themselves, and not one link to or from the other 294. They are not badly connected; they are a separate universe that happens to sit in the same Wikipedia category. Everyone else outside the giant component is alone: no links in either direction at all.

7. Checking the Snapshot Against Live Wikipedia

The snapshot is handed to us ready-made, so we re-derived a corner of it ourselves. The edges were harvested from the wiki-source, where internal links appear as [[Page name]] — so the same thing can be redone through the API and compared.

87 of 89 out-edges reproduced — 98%. Every edge our re-derivation found is in the snapshot; the snapshot has two that the current wiki-source does not (Hulk → Gargoyle, Adam Warlock → Quasar). We checked whether those were redirect artefacts and they are not, so the most likely explanation is that the two articles were edited after the 2026-08-26 freeze — but we have not confirmed that, and we would rather say so than invent a reason. The script is in the repo as scripts/check_snapshot_api.py if you want to re-run it.

What surprised us

1. The 17 isolates get all the attention, but 58 characters are never linked to. The dataset notes warn you about the 17 with no links in either direction. Nearly one in five characters has in-degree zero — they link out to others and nobody links back. Those are invisible to any centrality measure but still perfectly happy members of the graph.

2. Fame and talkativeness are nearly unrelated at the top. We expected the two leaderboards to broadly agree. Only 3 of 10 names overlap, and the biggest hub in the network links out less than the median character.

3. The island is a whole separate franchise. We went looking for badly connected stragglers and instead found a coherent 9-character cast from a 1986 series, densely linked to each other and hermetically sealed off from Marvel proper.
